<IntroParagraph>I am still in the early days of being a parent and balancing the much maligned “mental load” of motherhood. My toddler doesn't have much on his calendar, besides an inifinte loop of sleeping, eating, attending nursery, and manically running around the park squaking at pidegeons.</IntroParagraph>

And yet I am already feeling the mental load begin to bear down. I have a newly purchased house, one which is in fact a very old house, in need of a *lot* of rennovation work and maitenance. Alongside the standard stack of adult responsibilities: the dishes, the laundry, remembering which day recycling goes out, meal planning, grocery shopping, cleaning, paying whichever bills aren't on automated schedules, filing taxes, shuffling money around, scheduling with the babysitter, strategising kid-friendly weekend trips, birthday messages, planning when and how to see friends, buying the next size up toddler clothes before the old ones get too small, and the two dozen other ad hoc administative errands that always come up. Between a full-time job and a small child always in my arms, it is hard to make all this happen on time, if ever.

And I am still in the light years, on easy mode, with only one kid, and a partner who genuinely carries his share of the load (if not more).

We are rich in personal stories and cultural references about mothers with multiple school-aged children drowning in the hundreds of administrative tasks required of modern parenting: packing lunches, ferrying children to sports events, avoiding calendar conflicts, making ad-hoc costumes for surprise themed days at school, signing forms, planning birthday parties, making brownies for bake sales, coming up with screen-free weekend activites, etc. The show [Motherland](https://www.bbc.co.uk/iplayer/episodes/p05j1jkp/motherland) captures it well.

[As X and Y have pointed out, a large part of the problem is rising expectations. Families with personal au pairs and cleaners and private jets put themselves under increasing amounts of stress and the acceptable social bar raises higher]

Both the narratives we all hear and the hard data shows this is overwhelmingly mothers doing these kind of small, daily, keep-it-all-running tasks, as well as the ongoing cognitive work of planning ahead and anticipating everyone's needs. Of course there are families, like my own, where fathers carry an equal share. But the data shows there's just not enough of them for this to be a solved problem.

The best study I found is from Weeks & Ruppanner in 2025 surveying 3,000 U.S. parents on who typically completes which domestic tasks, with a follow-up study later that year over the same data.

Examples from my life: Buying a new washer-dryer took less than a hour. Tracking all the house rennovation work is done by Codex + Obsidian. Research, contractors, visualising rooms, learning about options. Tracking my son's current capabilites, the toy inventory, the book inventory, helping me research wtf montessori actually is, keep lists of simple activites to practice skills – often found on instagram, shared to codex, suggesting what new toys to buy at the right time, suggesting when its time to buy the next size up clothing (new ones every 6 months), calculating how many of each item to buy based on the UK climate, help me come up with activities for the weekend so he doesn't watch quite so much Miss Rachel.
